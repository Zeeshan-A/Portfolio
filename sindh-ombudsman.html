01 Context

What is the Sindh Ombudsman?

The Ombudsman's office investigates citizens' complaints against Sindh government departments. For 30 years, every complaint was filed, tracked and resolved on paper.

The new system had to work for three very different groups at once, from a citizen filing their first complaint on a phone to staff managing hearings across regional offices.

Citizens

File a complaint, then follow it to resolution without visiting an office.

Regional offices

Register, search and process complaints at the counter.

Case officers

Schedule hearings, run the cause list and dispose of cases in bulk.

02 Problem & research

How do you move a 30-year paper process online without losing anyone?

The challenge wasn't digitising forms. It was making a slow, opaque process feel quick and trustworthy to people who had only ever done it on paper.

Stakeholder sessionsPaper form auditCitizen interviewsProcess mapping
02 A

Registration was long and one-shot

The paper form asked for everything at once, in a single office visit. Citizens had to know which details mattered before they started, and one missing field could send them back.

02 B

No one could see where a complaint was

After filing, citizens heard nothing until a letter or hearing notice arrived. Checking status meant calling or visiting the office, and staff had to dig through files to answer.

02 C

Finding a case took too long

Staff searched handwritten registers and physical files by name or date. Finding one case meant asking colleagues and pulling folders, which slowed every hearing and reply.

02 D

Hearings and disposals were managed by hand

Cause lists were prepared by hand and disposals were recorded on paper, so senior officers had no live view of what was pending, overdue or escalated.

04 Solution

Wireframes first, then the shipped screens.

The final screens citizens and staff use today, designed and shipped across web, tablet and mobile.

H.01

Stepped registration

Three short screens instead of one long form: choose to log in or register, say who you are, then fill in only what's required. Optional details stay folded away, so a citizen can finish in about 3 minutes.

  • 01Two clear doorsLogin or Register, each explained in plain words.
  • 02The right form for each citizenPakistani, Overseas Pakistani or Foreigner.
  • 03Errors prevented up frontA NADRA phone hint shows before anyone types.
  • 04Required firstOptional information stays collapsed.
  • 05Urdu one tap awayA language switch from the very first screen.
H.02

One complaint, its whole story

Every complaint lives under one reference number and three tabs. Citizens see what they filed, everything that has happened since, and the final decision, without calling or visiting an office.

  • 01One reference numberRegion, department and year in one code, e.g. KHI-AK-EDU-0001/2025.
  • 02A numbered timelineWho acted, when, and with which documents.
  • 03Feedback when it closesCitizens rate the decision and add comments.
H.03

Insights at a glance

The first screen every officer sees. Seven numbers answer "how are we doing?", and two charts show where in Sindh complaints are slow or piling up. The same dashboard mirrors fully into Urdu, right to left.

  • 01Problems surface firstOverdue and escalated cases sit beside the totals, not in a report.
  • 02Where, not just how manyDistrict charts show which offices need help.
  • 03A true Urdu layoutNavigation, cards and charts all flip right to left.
  • 04Each role sees its own workA department officer gets a shorter menu and their complaint list.
H.04

Nine stages, one case page

The paper file became one page with nine tabs, in the order a case actually moves: Examination, Investigation, Decision Findings, Decision, Implementation, Representation, Disposal, Documents and Note Sheet. Officers always know which stage a case is in and what comes next.

  • 01Verify before admittingCNIC is verified first, then one clear choice: Admissible or Non-Admissible.
  • 02Drafts build on each otherFindings feed the decision, so nothing is retyped.
  • 03Reach people how they listenWhatsApp, SMS, email or post, chosen per update.
H.05

AI at four stages of every case

AI does the first draft at each stage of a case. Officials review and refine it instead of writing from scratch, so days of manual work become minutes.

AI Examination

3 days → 10 min

Runs the first examination of a complaint: reads the text, classifies its type and suggests jurisdiction and the relevant agency.

AI Findings Generator

Days → minutes

Reads the complaint and every investigation record, then generates a structured Findings document ready for review.

AI Decision Drafting

3 weeks → 2 days

Drafts a complete formal decision from the findings and case history. Officials refine it rather than write it.

AI Final Decision Refinement

Hours → minutes

Polishes the draft and checks it for consistency before the decision is officially announced.

H.06

Run by the office, not by developers

Roles, complaint categories and regional offices are all managed from the admin panel. When a department changes or a new office opens, staff update it themselves.

  • 01Access by roleCreate, update, view and delete, set per module.
  • 02Switch off, don't deleteToggles keep history intact when a category or office closes.

06 Learnings

What I'd take to the next project.

L.01

Urdu is a layout, not a translation

Planning right-to-left from the wireframes stopped Urdu from becoming a broken copy of English. Next time I’ll test RTL from day one, not after the English screens are done.

L.02

Measure from the start

The paper process had almost no data, so proving impact meant reconstructing numbers afterwards. On the next project I’d agree on baseline metrics in week one.
