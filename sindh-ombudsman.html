01 Context

What is the Sindh Ombudsman?

The office investigates citizens' complaints against provincial departments. For 30 years, every case moved on paper between offices. Three groups depend on the same file.

RoleWhat they needWhat paper made hard
CitizensFile a complaint and follow it to a decisionFiling meant a visit; after that, no way to check progress
Regional office staffRegister, find and update complaintsSearching handwritten registers by name or date
Case officersRun hearings, decide and close casesCause lists and disposals kept by hand, with no live view of what was pending

My part

The only designer, alongside a PM, a business analyst and developers. I owned the information architecture, designed every citizen and staff screen, built the component system, and ran handoff and design QA.

02 Problem

A complaint took 120 days on average, and nobody could see where it was.

Citizens couldn't follow their case, staff couldn't find it, and officers couldn't see what was overdue.

02 A

Filing took a visit and a long form

Everything was asked at once, in person.

02 B

After filing, silence

Checking progress meant a phone call or a visit.

02 C

Finding a case meant searching by hand

Handwritten registers, searched by name or date.

02 D

No live view for officers

Pending and overdue cases only showed up when someone counted them.

03 Investigation

Learning the paper process before designing a screen.

Citizens weren't part of the research, so we learned from the staff who handle every complaint. The product owner ran on-site sessions in Sukkur; I joined remotely.

MethodWith whomWhat it gave us

The findings became the four problems above, and the manuals' case order became the case page.

04 Design decisions

Each decision answers one of the four problems.

Each module went from wireframe and prototype to high fidelity, reviewed by the team and approved by the Ombudsman's office.

The citizen's happy path

22 wireframes first, then high fidelity. Here are 17 key screens in use today.

H.01

Stepped registration

Answers 02 A

Three short screens instead of one long form. Citizen type comes first, so the form asks only what that person needs, and optional details stay folded. Urdu is one tap away. About 3 minutes to file (estimated).

H.02

One complaint, one reference, three tabs

Answers 02 B

Each complaint gets a readable reference (region, department, year). The Activity tab shows who acted and when, answering "where is my case?" without a phone call. Citizens rate the decision when it closes.

H.03

Search and insights for staff

Answers 02 C and 02 D

Any case can be found by CNIC or complaint number from any screen. The role-based dashboard puts overdue and escalated counts beside the totals, shows which districts are falling behind, and mirrors fully into Urdu.

H.04

Nine stages, one case page

Answers 02 D

The paper file became one page with nine tabs, in the order the office's manuals set out. Officers kept their process; only where it lived changed.

  • 01Verify before admittingCNIC is checked first, so every admitted case is tied to a real person.
  • 02Drafts build on each otherFindings feed the decision, so nothing is retyped.
  • 03Reach people how they listenWhatsApp, SMS, email or post, because citizens use different channels.
H.05

Run by the office, not by developers

Staff manage roles, categories and offices themselves. Items are switched off, not deleted, so old cases keep their history.

05 Iteration

What staff changed.

Staff reviewed Figma prototypes at each stage. Two changes came straight from them.

I.01

Tabs instead of sub-menus

Module pages moved out of a long nested menu into tabs, so the menu stays short.

I.02

Multiple documents in one upload

Complaints often arrive with several papers, so the uploader takes them together.

Their effect on task time hasn't been measured.

07 Learnings

What I'd take to the next project.

L.01

Urdu is a layout, not a translation

Planning right to left from the wireframes worked. Next time, I'd review Urdu with staff in the same rounds as English.

L.02

Agree on the baseline in week one

Paper kept almost no data, so the "before" numbers were estimated. Next time I'd agree what to measure before launch.

L.03

Research through the people closest to the work

Staff were our best source. Remote sessions worked, but I'd observe the counter in person at least once.
