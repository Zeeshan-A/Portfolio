Case study · P. 03

US · Health & wellnessNew product · Mobile + Web

Nova Life: recovery support that doesn't stop at the clinic door.

NOVA LIFE, a US recovery centre, came with four ideas for an app and no first version. With a product owner, I helped turn them into an MVP scope, then designed the patient app and the therapist and admin portals. It shipped in 3 months and is used by 80+ of the centre's patients.

01 Context

What is NOVA LIFE?

NOVA LIFE runs rehabilitation programs and intensive care for people facing addiction, suicidal ideation and related challenges, with licensed providers and therapists. It wanted to keep supporting patients between sessions, not only inside its facilities.

RoleWhat they need from the product
PatientsSupport, content and check-ins between sessions
Therapists and providersA view of each patient's progress between visits
Admins and super adminManage patients, therapists and the app's content

My part

I was the only designer, working with the product owner and the developers. With the product owner I ran discovery with the client and mapped the user journeys; the product owner wrote the MVP scope. I proposed the sobriety counter, daily pledge and milestones, then designed the wireframes, the design system, the patient app and all three portals end to end.

02 Problem

A big vision, and no clear first version.

Between sessions, NOVA LIFE's patients had little support. The centre had four ideas to change that, but no agreed first release, so there was nothing a team could estimate or build. The first version had to serve patients, therapists and staff at once, and feel safe for people facing addiction or suicidal thoughts.

02 A

Four ideas, all preliminary

  • Idea 01On-demand contentVideo sessions, motivational and religious material, and therapy resources.
  • Idea 02AI-powered therapistAn empathetic AI companion for support at any hour.
  • Idea 03Periodic check-insWellness check-ins that track progress over time.
  • Idea 04Therapist accessA direct line to licensed providers and therapists.
02 B

Three users, one system

Patients, therapists and staff each needed something different, and every flow had to connect them.

02 C

A vulnerable audience

Calm and safe came before clever, especially for an AI companion.

03 Investigation

Discovery through the people who run the care.

Discovery was with NOVA LIFE's team and stakeholders. We didn't interview patients directly, for privacy and clinical-access reasons, so the centre's staff spoke for them.

Over several meetings, the product owner and I worked through three questions.

QuestionWhat we learned
What happens when a patient comes in?Patients join the app when they're onboarded at the clinic in person, so the app picks up where the intake leaves off
What does the first release need?All four of the client's ideas: the content library, Nova AI, check-ins through video surveys, and the therapist portal. Live classes and the Canary wellness insights were left for a later release
How does each user move through the product?User journeys for all three groups, built from the meetings and the client's requirements document
03 A

The big picture, in screens

I turned each round of notes into low-fidelity wireframes, so the client could react to screens rather than descriptions. The whole app was then walked through screen by screen, and the client approved the big picture before any high-fidelity work.

04 Design decisions

Calm, simple and low effort, from the first screen.

D.01

A calm foundation

A calm teal primary, soft neutrals, a condensed title face over a readable body face, and a small set of components every screen uses. For people in a hard moment, the interface shouldn't add noise.

D.02

Onboarding by conversation

Instead of a 31-question intake form, the AI companion asks out loud and the patient answers. Patients vary widely, and clinic assistants often sit with them during onboarding, so a conversation is easier for both. Patients can pause at any time and review every answer before it's saved.

D.03

Private, but quick to reopen

A one-time code, then a 4-digit PIN with an optional hint, and Face ID or Touch ID. The app holds sensitive health data, so it locks, but getting back in takes a second.

D.04

Progress you can see

Home leads with days sober and the next milestone. Milestone badges, from 7 days to a year, each have a name and a celebration. Resetting the counter is framed gently ("This moment counts"), so a relapse doesn't feel like failure in the app. The library holds short guided sessions for breath, balance and faith.

D.05

A pledge each morning

Each morning the patient swipes to recommit, with their own reasons in front of them. I proposed the pledge, the counter and the milestones as a set; the product owner approved them, then the client.

D.06

Help for the hard moments

Nova AI answers by text or voice, and opens with quick prompts like "I had a craving today".

D.07

Profile and reminders

Daily reminders for the pledge and review, downloads for offline sessions, and the support and emergency options always one tap away.

D.08

Safety and privacy

For people facing addiction or suicidal thoughts, the app has to be safe before it is clever.

  • 01Help at any hourA 24/7 emergency button connects patients to support straight away.
  • 02An AI with clinical guardrailsThe AI companion was set up for the client's clinical and legal requirements.
  • 03Consent firstPatients give consent during onboarding at the clinic, including for voice answers and for therapists seeing AI summaries of their chats.
  • 04Private by designPatient data is handled under HIPAA and stays with NOVA LIFE.
P.01

Portals: admin

Staff sign in to see patient and therapist accounts at a glance, review therapists, and manage the menus, series and videos patients see in the app.

P.02

Portals: super admin

The super admin invites patients by link, email or QR code, and approves the therapists who sign up, each with their license, documents and a clear status.

P.03

Portals: the care team between sessions

Therapists verify their license to join, invite patients, then follow each one between sessions: days sober, how they use the app, survey answers, wellness trends and an AI summary of their chats, which patients consent to at the clinic.

05 Iteration

From a form to a conversation.

I.01

Onboarding

The first version was a standard intake form, with input fields and tap-to-select pills. Patients vary widely in how comfortable they are with forms, and clinic assistants often sit with them during onboarding. We rebuilt it as a spoken conversation with the AI companion, with pauses and a review step.

I.02

The pledge cards

In review, the client shared a set of spiritual shapes to use on the daily pledge cards, and the pledge design was updated around them.

07 Learnings

What I'd take to the next project.

L.01

Shape the first release before the first screen

Agreeing the scope before high fidelity turned four big ideas into something a team could build in 3 months.

L.02

A conversation beats a form for this audience

Typing answers or tapping selection pills was harder for patients and the assistants helping them. Speaking was designed to keep a 31-question intake manageable, with breaks and a review step.

L.03

Push for safe access to real users

Without direct access to patients, the client's team had to speak for them. Next time I'd look for a safe way to hear from a few patients, for example through their therapists.
