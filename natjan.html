NATJAN: a vendor portal redesigned, without starting over.

A US facilities company's vendor portal had every feature but no guidance. As the only designer, I redesigned it on a themed version of its existing MUI library, without a rebuild, and designed the core flows of a new mobile app.

01 Context

What is NATJAN?

NATJAN is The Facilities Group's janitorial management system: a vendor portal, an internal portal and an on-site mobile app.

The vendor portal worked, on a stock MUI library. The brief: make it clearer without throwing that away, and design the new app's core flows.

RoleWhereWhat they do
VendorsWeb portal and mobile appReceive work orders, log crew visits, upload documents, invoice, and choose how fast they get paid
Area ManagersMobile appInspect sites in their area, including unplanned visits
Regional ManagersMobile appInspect across their region, with the same flow as Area Managers

My part

The only designer, with a PM, a scrum master, the client's PO and developers. I audited the portal, led the technical study that chose theming over rebuilding, proposed the new IA, and designed the portal across 5 breakpoints plus the mobile flows.

02 MUI Customization

Theme MUI, don't replace it.

After a technical study with the developers, we kept MUI and restyled it: new theme, tokens and components. Features kept running, developers kept their APIs, and one theme covered 300+ screens.

  • TypographyRobotoInstrument Sans
  • Corner radiusSharp 4px cornersSofter 8px on cards, buttons, dialogs and inputs
  • ColourStock MUI paletteNATJAN's primary colour as the main theme
  • Shadows and bordersDefault MUI elevationSubtler, softer shadows and lighter borders
  • Buttons and inputsDefault MUI stylingSame sizing, with the new radius and primary colour
  • IconsMaterial IconsLucide, an open-source icon library

03 Problem

The portal worked. It just didn't guide anyone.

Vendors had every tool, but no sense of what mattered next. The fix had to happen without a rebuild.

02 A

QuickPay: a money decision buried in small print

QuickPay trades a fee for faster payment, yet vendors had to commit without seeing the options or costs. They raised it in their regular meetings with the client.

  1. 1
    The rates were hidden

    All five payment terms sat inside one dropdown, so vendors couldn't compare them before choosing.

  2. 2
    Consent came before the choice

    A paragraph of small italic legal text and a checkbox had to be dealt with first.

  3. 3
    A button that stayed off

    Opt-in stayed disabled until both were done, with nothing saying why.

02 B

A cluttered navigation

Single items opened sub-menus, labels were long, and secondary items mixed with the main tasks.

02 C

No way to record an ad-hoc inspection

Managers visit sites without a work order, but had no mobile flow to record those inspections.

02 D

Work orders with no overview

The list opened at 1,000 rows, with grid tools above the data and no summary of what was open or overdue.

04 Investigation

Three sources, one list of fixes.

I didn't have direct access to vendors, so the evidence came from three places.

SourceWhat it gave us
My audit of the existing portalUsability issues by module, each linked to a Jira change request
Vendor feedback, through the clientVendors' frustration points. QuickPay came up most
Client meetingsManagers needed ad-hoc inspections: the first mobile flow

The technical study set the main constraint: theme MUI, don't replace it.

05 Design decisions

Same portal, clearer at every step.

D.01

A full UI overhaul, built for data

Answers 02 B and 02 D

Features stayed, but every screen was rebuilt around one idea: the data is the work, so the frame stays quiet. Drag the line to compare.

  • 01A dark frame, a light workspaceNavy navigation, white workspace: two clear zones, one focus at a time.
  • 02KPIs before rowsOpen, due, completed and NTE value cards come before the table.
  • 03A simpler navigationItems open directly, labels are short ("Work orders", not "List of work orders"), secondary items sit at the bottom. I proposed it in client meetings and we refined it in Figma.
  • 04A cleaner, calmer UIOne toolbar, colour tags, 12 rows per page instead of 1,000, Lucide icons.
D.02

QuickPay: choose, then confirm

Answers 02 A

All five terms are visible to compare. The legal text became three plain points after the choice, and a confirmation shows the fee on a $1,000 invoice. By design, opt-in takes 3 clicks instead of 4.

AspectBeforeAfter
Payment termsFive rates hidden in one dropdownAll five visible as options, one preselected
ConsentItalic legal paragraph and a checkbox, before anything elseThree plain-language points, after the choice
CostA percentage onlyA worked example on a $1,000 invoice
WarningsP.O. box rule buried in the legal textIts own highlighted warning
Clicks to opt in4: tick, open dropdown, pick, Opt-in3: pick a term, Opt-in, Agree and opt-in
D.03

One work order, one page

Information, recurrence, IVR, crew, notes and documents each get a tab, so vendors work from one page.

D.04

Multiple breakpoints, one overview

Google Analytics showed vendors on many screen sizes, so the overview works at 5 breakpoints: icons on tablet, a menu on phone, and cards that stack.

D.05

Mobile: inspections for Area and Regional Managers

Answers 02 C

Find a nearby site, check in within 100 m so the visit is verified, run the checklist and check out with a summary. Area and Regional Managers share one flow.

D.06

Mobile: a guided vendor visit

The app recommends the next work order, verifies the vendor is on site, then walks them through photos, notes, a checklist and an e-signature. Check-out unlocks only when proof of work is complete.

D.07

Checklists: one question at a time

Each question gets its own screen, with a step bar and a review before submitting.

06 Iteration

Module by module, through client review.

Every module went through the same loop, tracked in Jira.

I.01

The review loop

  1. 01
    Analyze the existing portal

    Issues and inconsistencies, each linked to a change request.

  2. 02
    Plan the improvements

    New patterns, consistent with the design system.

  3. 03
    Client review

    Present, gather feedback, revise until approved.

  4. 04
    Handoff

    Figma files, specs and assets for the developers.

New features added wireframes and a second approval before handoff.

I.02

One pin colour became a map key

The map first used one pin colour. Review showed managers needed to tell work orders from inspections at a glance, so pins are now colour-coded, with a legend.

07 Outcomes

A familiar portal, now built to guide.

08 Learnings

What I'd take to the next project.

L.01

Customizing can beat rebuilding

Theming MUI changed the experience and kept working code. Agreeing it with the developers first kept every screen buildable.

L.02

Agree on a number before launch

Next time I'd agree one number before launch, like the QuickPay opt-in rate, so the improvement can be shown, not just seen.
